## ftue-data

### Резюме
FTUE benchmarks for 2024-2026 converge on a clear picture for a casual tap-away puzzle like Maze Arrows. Median mobile D1 retention has slid to ~22% (GameAnalytics 2026, 16,000+ games), while puzzle-genre D1 sits at ~32% and top-decile games hold ~40% — so 32% is par and 40% is the realistic stretch target. Most of D1 is decided inside the first session: players judge a game within 5-15 minutes, a "quick win" must land within roughly 60-90 seconds of launch, and healthy tutorial completion is 70-80% (with ~20% of players failing to finish even the first quest in DeltaDNA's cross-game data). The strongest experimental evidence (Andersen et al., 45,000 players) shows tutorials add up to +29% playtime only in complex games and add nothing significant in simple ones whose mechanics are discoverable by tapping — which Maze Arrows' one-verb core loop is — while A/B data shows adding a skip option lifts D1 by +4-11% relative. Interactive learn-by-doing consistently beats text/slides (2-3x engagement; contextual just-in-time teaching outperforms front-loaded instruction). For permissions: gaming ATT opt-in averages ~31-35% (Q2 2025), push opt-in for casual games can be as low as 23% on iOS, and pre-permission priming screens shown after a value moment (first win) lift opt-ins 2-3x versus cold prompts at launch. Net design directive: the tutorial should BE the first 2-3 real levels with contextual ghost-step hints and a guaranteed sub-90-second first flying-arrow win, skippable, with all permission prompts and the multi-page paywall sequenced after that first win.

### Факты
- Median (P50) mobile game D1 retention fell to ~22% in 2025 data; top 25% (P75) is just above 30%, top 10% (P90) ~40%, top 1% (P99) 64-68%. Session length: P75 ~5.2 min, P90 ~8 min, P99 ~22 min. Sample: 16,000+ games, 9 regions, iOS+Android. [GameAnalytics 2026 Mobile & PC Gaming Benchmarks (via Gamigion summary), 2026 | https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks]
- Puzzle genre retention benchmarks: D1 31.85%, D7 12.18%, D30 5.35%; adjacent Match genre leads with D1 32.65%, D7 13.98%, D30 7.15%. Puzzle/board/card are the strongest genres for medium/long-term retention despite arcade leading on D1. [Segwise 2026 retention guide (compiling Mistplay / Adjust / Business of Apps data), 2026 | https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies]
- iOS outperforms Android on FTUE outcomes: top-25% games see D1 of 31-33% on iOS vs 25-27% on Android (2024 full-year data, 11,600 games) — relevant since Maze Arrows is iOS-only, so benchmarks should be read against the higher iOS bar. [GameAnalytics 2025 Mobile Gaming Benchmarks (via GameDev Reports), 2025 | https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks]
- Controlled experiment across 45,000 players, 3 games, 8 tutorial designs: tutorials increased play time up to +29% in the most complex game but produced NO significant engagement gain in the two simpler games — 'investment in tutorials may not be justified for games with mechanics that can be discovered through experimentation'. Context-sensitive teaching (at the moment of use) improved tutorial efficiency. [Andersen et al., 'The Impact of Tutorials on Games of Varying Complexity', CHI (classic academic anchor, still the best experimental evidence), 2012 | https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/]
- A/B tests aggregated from GDC/Unity sources: adding a SKIP option to the tutorial produced a +4-11% relative increase in D1 retention, provided tutorial info remains retrievable later (help menu, contextual tips). [StatFacts insight on tutorial skip options, 2024 | https://statfacts.net/insight/tutorial-skip-option-games_en]
- Counter-nuance: tutorial presence positively impacts NON-EXPERT players' flow and continuous-use intentions in mobile games — so the answer is not 'no tutorial' but a skippable, invisible one embedded in play. [MDPI Multimodal Technologies and Interaction, 'Demystifying the First-Time Experience of Mobile Games', 2020 | https://www.mdpi.com/2414-4088/4/3/41]
- ~20% of players do not complete even the first quest/tutorial step (DeltaDNA cross-game analysis); some players churn BEFORE reaching the tutorial due to load times/crashes — first-load performance is part of FTUE. [Udonis FTUE guide (citing DeltaDNA), 2024 | https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/first-time-user-experience]
- Healthy tutorial completion benchmark is 70-80% of starters; ~52% onboarding drop-off is 'average'; if 70% drop before completing onboarding the tutorial is too long or confusing. devtodev tooling exists specifically to find per-step drop-off. [devtodev analytics guides, 2025 | https://www.devtodev.com/resources/articles/getting-started-with-analytics-a-step-by-step-guide-for-developers]
- Time-to-first-gameplay should be under 60 seconds from install (no settings/account screens first), and the 'quick win' must happen within the first ~90 seconds; practitioners treat ~70% opening-session completion as the healthy threshold and note a ~3-minute window on mobile where early losses become churn events. [Recognizing Patterns — 'First Time User Experience: The 60 Seconds That Decide' + Hubapps retention guide, 2025 | https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/first-time-user-experience-your-player]
- Players decide within the first 5-15 minutes of session 1 whether the game is worth their time; 60% of players quit games that become too hard too quickly — early difficulty spikes are the top first-session drop-off cause alongside long tutorials and unclear goals. [80.lv 'How to Solve Player Drop-Off in Mobile Game Design' / Segwise, 2025 | https://80.lv/articles/how-to-solve-player-drop-off-in-modern-mobile-game-design]
- Scale of day-0/day-1 loss: ~77% of users churn on the first day for the average app, and roughly 70-85% of mobile game installers never return after the first day (Playnomics ~85%) — D1 is effectively won or lost inside session 1. [Mistplay churn guide / maf.ad retention benchmarks, 2025 | https://maf.ad/en/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks/]
- AFK Arena reached 53% D1 retention (vs ~24% cross-genre average) largely via a time-limited guided tutorial engineered around guaranteed early victories — the canonical case for 'rig the first win'. [Keewano FTUE blog / Udonis, 2025 | https://keewano.com/blog/first-time-user-experience-ftue-mobile-games/]
- Interactive learn-by-doing formats drive 2-3x higher engagement than static/text content, and interactive tutorials improve knowledge retention 25-67% vs passive formats; game-industry consensus (Clash Royale pattern) is playable guided tutorials over text slides. [Segwise / iSpring / Udonis mobile game tutorial guide, 2025 | https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/mobile-game-tutorial]
- Session benchmarks for pacing session 1: top-25% games average 8-9 min per session (median 5-6 min), ~4 sessions/day; casual top-25-by-revenue D1 ~30% per Sensor Tower — a first session of ~8-12 min covering 3-5 levels matches top-quartile behavior. [GameAnalytics 2025 benchmarks / Sensor Tower via Stepico, 2025 | https://stepico.com/blog/mobile-gaming-has-entered-its-retention-era-what-sensor-tower-data-means/]
- ATT opt-in Q2 2025: industry average 35% of users shown the prompt consent (up from 34.5% in 2024); gaming averages 31% vs non-gaming 42%; by genre: sports 50%, hypercasual 43%, action 40%, board 30%. AppsFlyer separately reports >45% of users who SEE the prompt consent and 80% of apps have implemented ATT. [Adjust ATT 2025 benchmarks + AppsFlyer, 2025 | https://www.adjust.com/blog/att-opt-in-rates-2025/]
- ATT timing: AppsFlyer notes highest raw opt-in when prompted at first launch, but apps that delay the prompt until users experience a benefit 'earn more yeses over time'; recommended pattern is a custom pre-ATT priming screen (e.g., Nike shows it at the END of onboarding) explaining the value before the system dialog. [AppsFlyer 'The ATT prompt: why and when to show it' / 'How apps boost ATT opt-in with pre-prompts', 2024 | https://www.appsflyer.com/blog/tips-strategy/apps-boost-att-opt-in/]
- Push notifications: all-industry iOS opt-in averages ~54-56%, but hypercasual/casual games average only 23% on iOS; pre-permission priming (custom in-app soft-ask BEFORE the native dialog, fired only if the user agrees) improves opt-in 2-3x, and the ask should come after a value moment (first win/first success), never during cold onboarding. [Plotline / PushEngage / OneSignal push opt-in guides, 2025 | https://www.plotline.so/blog/how-to-improve-push-notification-opt-in-rates]
- Effective onboarding is repeatedly credited with up to +50% retention improvement (practitioner claim, multiple vendors), and 67.1% of players say good game progression is why they keep playing — early progression clarity (stars, chapter map) is itself a retention lever. [Keewano (citing Business of Apps) / Medium onboarding UX roundup, 2025 | https://keewano.com/blog/first-time-user-experience-ftue-mobile-games/]

### Выводы для дизайна
Concrete spec for Maze Arrows onboarding: (1) Cold start straight into level 1 — no logo carousel, no settings, no account, no text slides; target under 10s from tap-icon to interactive board and under 90s to first level-complete. Level 1 must be unloseable (every piece clearable in any order, or lives disabled for levels 1-3). (2) The tutorial IS levels 1-3: level 1 teaches tap→fly with a single pulsing piece (reuse runHintFeedback pulse + runFlyOut squash/particle juice — the existing juice is the reward); level 2 introduces blocking with one intentionally blocked piece so the player safely experiences the red-flash/shake feedback without losing progress; level 3 introduces order-thinking and fires the first combo callout ('Chain Clear!') plus runVictoryBurst. Teach each mechanic at the moment it's needed (context-sensitivity beats front-loading, per Andersen). (3) Add a 'Skip intro' affordance from level 1 for genre-savvy tap-away players (+4-11% D1), with mechanics re-learnable via the existing hint system — which doubles as the always-available help menu, satisfying the skip-option precondition. (4) During level 2-3, surface one free auto-hint moment ('Stuck? Watch this' → ghost-step) to demonstrate the hint system BEFORE the paywall, since hints are a core subscription perk and 55% of trial cancels happen day 0 — the paywall after level 2-3 then sells something already experienced; tease Photo Maze on the paywall's multi-page flow rather than in-tutorial (too heavy for the 90-second window). (5) Permission sequence: no prompts at launch; ATT pre-prompt (custom screen, value-framed) after the level-3 victory burst and before/around the paywall; push soft-ask (2-3x lift) only after the first session's last win, framed around lives refill/daily quest — expect ~23% baseline without priming, 40-60% with. (6) Instrument the funnel from day one: per-step tutorial events (target ≥70-80% completion of levels 1-3, alarm if any single step loses >10%), time-to-first-win (<90s), session-1 length (target 8-12 min, 3-5 levels), and D1 by cohort (par 32%, stretch 40% — iOS-only means judge against the higher iOS bar). (7) Difficulty guard: no fail-possible level before level 4-5 (60% quit games that get hard too fast); first life-loss should happen only after the player has banked 3+ wins.

## teardowns

### Резюме
Across 2024-2026 teardowns of the casual-puzzle leaders, the winning first-session pattern is "instant board, zero menus": Block Blast opens straight into play with no tutorial wall, Two Dots teaches by a ~1-minute gesture-based learn-by-doing intro, and Royal Match is praised specifically for best-in-class time-to-fun (no loading screens, minimal-friction core loop, objectives explained inside the level). Tutorials in this genre are level-design-as-teacher plus a contextual pointer, not tutorial rooms or text walls; GameRefinery explicitly flags tutorial rooms as ineffective for casual. Early levels are engineered as guaranteed wins (Zen Match makes the first wave of every level solvable in under a minute; the general FTUE literature pushes unloseable early beats because ~20% of players drop before finishing the first tutorial quest). Monetization is deliberately held out of the first minutes: Color Block Jam starts ad revenue only after level 14 (later builds reported ad-free for ~50 levels) and introduces its first IAP (the "Failed Level Pack") only at the first difficulty spike; Royal Match ran with zero ads for five years as a brand promise and only added interstitial+rewarded ads on May 11, 2026 for Blended-ROAS UA reasons. Ad-side discipline is codified: gate the first interstitial on levels cleared / sessions / time-since-install, fire only on level transitions, dual-axis frequency caps (seconds AND levels), win/loss states tracked separately, everything remote-configured — Block Blast tuned exactly this with ~10,000 A/B tests in 2025 while holding retention above genre average at 70M DAU. Meta systems, lives pressure, and currencies are drip-fed well after session one (Royal Match: Teams at level 21, Royal Pass at 37, Collection at 41, locked areas visible as foreshadowing). No top casual puzzle game in any teardown uses a personalization quiz — the genre substitute is a difficulty picker (Sudoku.com's six tiers). Honest gaps: no published FTUE teardowns exist for Woodoku, Sudoku.com, Pixel Art, or Tap Away/Block Away specifically — for those only app-store review evidence and genre-level analyses are available.

### Факты
- Block Blast opens directly into gameplay with no tutorial wall or menus; its rules are learned in the first round of play. It was the most-downloaded mobile game in the world in 2024 and 2025, crossed 870M lifetime downloads, and reached 70M DAU / 300M MAU by late 2025 (up from 40M DAU / 160M MAU in Dec 2024), with retention above casual-industry average and 'user retention and playtime' as the studio's stated north-star metrics. [Mobilegamer.biz — Block Blast maker Hungry Studio A/B tested its way to the top / data digest, 2025 | https://mobilegamer.biz/block-blast-maker-hungry-studio-a-b-tested-its-way-to-the-top-of-the-charts/]
- Block Blast is monetized entirely by ads (no IAP), earning an estimated $584k/day (~$17.5M/month, other estimates $1M/day), and Hungry Studio ran ~10,000 A/B tests in 2025 (50+ tests weekly, 97% failure rate) much of it tuning ad placement and frequency to hold retention while maximizing revenue; VP Jason Wang says they 'intentionally avoid designing mechanics that create excessive urgency or high-pressure time constraints' and accept short-term negative ROI for long-term users. [Gamesforum — How Block Blast! Shattered Records; Mobilegamer.biz; Udonis Block Blast statistics, 2025 | https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/how-block-blast-shattered-records-with-ai-a-b-testing-and-a-bold-bet-on-long-term-players]
- Color Block Jam (Gybe Games, biggest new puzzle breakout of 2025, ~$150k/day and growing) keeps onboarding light: ~12 intro levels with a quick tutorial then player-led discovery, and ad revenue begins only after level 14 per Felix Braberg's analysis; a later Gamesforum play-through reported being 'at least 50 levels in' with no ads, then banners as a soft sell, then interstitials after level completions/failures — a deliberate staged-monetization ramp. [Gamesforum — Color Block Jam: The Art of Monetization in Stages; Gamigion/Felix Braberg podcast, 2025 | https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/color-block-jam-the-art-of-monetization-in-stages]
- Color Block Jam's first in-app purchase offer, the 'Failed Level Pack', is introduced only when players start hitting difficulty spikes and failing levels — the first monetization moment is tied to the first felt need, not to a calendar or level counter. [Gamesforum — Color Block Jam: The Art of Monetization in Stages, 2025 | https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/color-block-jam-the-art-of-monetization-in-stages]
- Royal Match ran with ZERO ads for its entire first five years as an explicit brand promise ('no in-game ads' was a UA pillar), monetizing purely via IAP (~$130M/month, $3B+ lifetime) — 'every frustration in the game has exactly one relief valve, and it's the IAP store'; Dream Games only added interstitial and rewarded ads on May 11, 2026, after sister title Royal Kingdom's March 2026 ad launch produced ~20% incremental revenue (~$185k/day) and enabled AppLovin Blended-ROAS UA campaigns. [Felix Braberg Substack — Dream Games Brings Interstitial Ads to Royal Match; Udonis Royal Match analysis; FoxAdvert, 2026 | https://felixbraberg.substack.com/p/dream-games-brings-interstitial-ads]
- Royal Match's onboarding is 'minimal friction' by design per Deconstructor of Fun: fastest switcher live, no visible loading screens, no multi-board levels, objectives (e.g. clear grass) explained inside the level itself, all early levels beatable without boosters or spending, generous power-ups, and the board shuffles instead of forcing power-up use — 'time to reach the fun is best-in-class'. [Deconstructor of Fun — Royal Match: The New King from Turkey?; Naavik Royal Match deep dive, 2021-2024 | https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2021/3/21/royal-match-the-new-king-from-turkey]
- Royal Match drip-feeds systems far past session one: Teams unlock at level 21, Royal Pass (battle pass) at level 37, Collection at level 41; upcoming areas are visible but locked as foreshadowing; the early 'Butler's Gift' (free bundled power-ups at level start for up to 3 levels) uses loss aversion as the first soft monetization touch; no written narrative slows the first session down. [Udonis — How Does Royal Match Make Money; Naavik — Royal Match: Dream Games' Regal Performance, 2024-2025 | https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/royal-match-analysis]
- Two Dots teaches via a ~1-minute gesture-based learn-by-doing sequence (connect dots, get next practice set, pace adapts to player speed), with no text-heavy instructions, square-mechanic held until level 3, and login fully skippable; in the cited 12-user test, the onboarding prototype converted 16.67% while no-onboarding users stalled on the first game screen trying to figure out mechanics. [Medium (The User Onboarding Journal) — Analysing on-boarding experience of Two Dots, 2019 (pattern still current) | https://medium.com/welcome-aboard/analysing-on-boarding-experience-of-a-gaming-app-two-dots-936c9eef7de2]
- Zen Match structures EVERY level as two waves where the first wave is intentionally easy and solvable in under a minute — a built-in guaranteed-success beat for new players; Deconstructor of Fun notes it only becomes tedious for veterans and suggests removing it around level 50-100, i.e. it exists specifically for onboarding; meta is a light room-coloring collection fed by level points. Genre norm once ramped: interstitial after each ~2-minute level, 6-9 impressions per 15-minute session. [Felix Braberg Substack / Gamigion — 7 Best Practices for Interstitial Ads in Level-Based Games, 2024-2025 | https://felixbraberg.substack.com/p/7-best-practices-for-interstitial]

[Udonis — First-Time User Experience (FTUE) In Mobile Games, 2024 | https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/first-time-user-experience]

### Выводы для дизайна
Concrete blueprint for Maze Arrows onboarding, grounded in the teardowns: (1) COLD OPEN — on first launch skip MainMenuView entirely and load Level 1's board within seconds; the world map, 6 modes, themes, and quests must not exist for a new install until after level 3 (Block Blast / Royal Match time-to-fun pattern). (2) LEVEL DESIGN AS TEACHER, 3 levels — Level 1: 3-5 pieces, every piece free-to-fly, a pulsing hand cursor on the first correct piece (reuse existing hint pipeline: highlightArea → piece pulse → runGhostStep is literally the genre-standard contextual-pointer stack); every tap fires runFlyOut squash + particle trail so the core verb is self-rewarding. Level 2: first intentionally blocked piece to teach the red-flash rule — but with no life penalty yet (Zen Match guaranteed-first-wave pattern; AFK unloseable-early-beats pattern; ~20% of players quit tutorials that punish). Level 3: first mini-chain to trigger ComboBurstView + 'Chain Clear!' callout and runVictoryBurst — the celebration beat every winner places in minute one. (3) SYSTEMS DRIP — introduce lives only when classic mode starts (post-paywall, ~level 4+), stars at first level-complete screen, coins/currencies later still; show locked chapters on the map as foreshadowing (Royal Match locked-areas pattern). (4) VALUE-BEFORE-PAYWALL — during levels 2-3 grant one free auto-hint ('this is what unlimited hints feels like') and show a 15-second Photo Maze teaser (generate from a bundled sample photo, not the user's), so the day-0 multi-page paywall pitches perks the player has already touched — this is the Color Block Jam 'first offer at first felt need' principle adapted to a subscription. (5) AD GUARDRAILS — no ads at all in levels 1-3 and never before the paywall; first interstitial gated on (level ≥ 8-10) AND (≥ 4-5 min since install), fired only on level-complete/fail transitions, dual-axis cooldown (e.g. ≥ 90s AND ≥ 2 levels since last ad), softer treatment after failed levels, all thresholds in remote config for A/B tests. (6) NO QUIZ — skip any personalization survey (zero genre precedent); if segmentation is wanted, a single 'Relax / Challenge' choice after level 3 is the genre-native form (Sudoku.com difficulty-picker pattern). (7) Zen Match's decline is the warning: cap session-start popups to one (the paywall) — daily quests, weekly challenge, and achievement toasts must not stack on the first sessions or they poison the relax positioning.

## paywall-onboarding

### Резюме
Research across Superwall, RevenueCat, Adapty, growth.design and games-industry sources (2024-2026) strongly validates the already-decided multi-page onboarding paywall and clarifies exactly how to build it. Superwall's 40M-open study (Feb-May 2026) shows multi-page onboarding paywalls convert 12.41% vs 9.07% for single-page (+37%), with the recommended order: value proposition → expectation-setting/trial timeline → pricing → payment, in 2-4 screens. The Blinkist-style trial timeline (Today / Day 2 reminder / Day 3 billing) is the highest-evidence trust pattern: +23% trial starts, -55% complaints, +1,200% notification opt-in. Critically, the famous trial-toggle pattern is now OFF the table for iOS: Apple has been systematically rejecting toggle paywalls under Guideline 3.1.2 since mid-January 2026, despite its historical +17% ARPU / +72% install-to-trial data — Maze Arrows is iOS-only, so the timeline paywall is the compliant substitute. Price display best practice is per-period reframing (annual shown as $/week lifts annual-plan adoption ~28-34%) plus a "Save X%" badge (+20-40% annual adoption) and a default-highlighted plan (default selection shifts 35-45% of buyers to that plan vs 15-20%). For the decline flow, the consensus 2026 pattern is: nothing discounted on the main paywall (9 in 10 subscriptions sell at full price), but a time-limited 24h "welcome offer" shown only AFTER the user closes the onboarding paywall, worth an expected 10-15% ARPU; Superwall's abandoned-transaction paywalls drove 17% of total revenue in one case study. Quiz-style personalization has strong app evidence (Noom >10% quiz-completer conversion vs 2.7% median) but essentially zero evidence in games — games use playable gameplay funnels instead, so Maze Arrows' 2-3 juicy tutorial levels ARE its investment-builder and should not be replaced by a quiz. Copy that converts: benefit-phrased bulleted value stacks with action verbs, social proof placed above price tiers, soft-commitment CTA ("Start My Free Trial", "Cancel anytime", explicit billing date) — though Adapty's test data shows copy/visual-only tests have the lowest win rate (34.6%) vs structural tests (trial structure 59.6%, plan count 57.1%), so structure > polish.

## psychology

### Резюме
First-session psychology research (2016-2026) converges on a few hard numbers. The first 5-10 minutes decide retention: bottom-quartile games lose 46-58% of installs in that window vs 24% for top performers (Google Play data), and games whose first session exceeds the 9-minute mean see 31% D1 retention vs 20% below it (deltaDNA, 275 games). Difficulty data from real puzzle games (Lily's Garden/Tactile 2024, King GDC 2024) confirms the folklore: first ~100 levels are deliberately one-attempt easy (~100% win rate), stabilizing at ~3.2 attempts later; early fail spikes (e.g., 99% pass L1 but 65% L3) are a named churn driver. Celebration "juice" is now quantified: Kao's 3,018-participant study found an inverted-U — medium/high juiciness significantly increases playtime, intrinsic motivation and performance, while both zero and extreme juice decrease all three. Endowed progress is genuinely measured (Nunes & Drèze: 34% vs 19% completion with 2 pre-stamped steps), and cross-app platform data shows users who complete one achievement on day 0 retain 33.4% vs 20.4% at D14 (+64% relative). Early customization/IKEA-effect data is directional but consistent (Drift doubled onboarding length by adding customization and improved retention; academic reviews link avatar/visual customization to identification and retention). Zeigarnik/cliffhanger design remains lab-validated but game-folklore in application — no public game A/B exists. Loss aversion in trials has one clean case: Blinkist's transparent "we'll remind you before trial ends" paywall lifted trial starts 23% and push opt-in from 6% to 74%. For streaks, freeze-protected streaks run 48% longer (17.2 vs 11.6 days), supporting day-0 streak introduction with forgiveness mechanics built in.

### Факты
- MEASURED (field experiment): Endowed progress — car-wash loyalty cards requiring 8 stamps had 19% completion; cards requiring 10 stamps but pre-stamped with 2 (same real effort) reached 34% completion, and holders completed faster as they neared the goal (goal-gradient acceleration). This is the canonical Nunes & Drèze study behind 'start the progress bar above zero'. [Nunes & Drèze, 'The Endowed Progress Effect', Journal of Consumer Research (via SSRN), 2006 | https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=991962]
- MEASURED (188 SaaS companies): average onboarding-checklist completion is only 19.2%; best practice that lifts it: keep to ≤7 steps, add a progress bar, and include tasks the user has ALREADY completed (applied endowed progress). Progress-bar prominence compounds the lift and checklist completers convert to paid at higher rates. [Userpilot Onboarding Checklist Completion Rate Benchmark Report, 2024-2025 | https://userpilot.com/blog/onboarding-checklist-completion-rate-benchmarks/]
- MEASURED (n=3,018, largest juiciness study to date): four identical action-RPG builds with None/Medium/High/Extreme juice — Medium and High juiciness significantly increased play time, intrinsic motivation, player experience AND performance; both None and Extreme significantly decreased all of them. Juice impact is an inverted-U, not 'more is better'. [Kao, 'The Effects of Juiciness in an Action RPG', Entertainment Computing, 2020 | https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1875952118300879]

### Выводы для дизайна
Concrete design for Maze Arrows day-0 onboarding, mapped to evidence: (1) Difficulty curve — levels 1-3 must be one-attempt wins (~100% win rate, matching Lily's Garden production data); teach the 'blocked piece' red-flash mechanic in a sandboxed moment that costs NO life the first time; do not allow a possible fail until level 4-5, and instrument per-level win rate watching for cliffs (99%→65% pattern = churn). Define the activation metric now — proposal: 'completed 3 levels AND triggered ≥1 combo in session 1' — and validate against D7 cohorts. (3) Endowed progress — replace or augment the world map intro with a 5-item 'First Steps' checklist whose first 2 items auto-complete during tutorial (bar starts at 40%); remaining items: pick your theme, use a hint, start your streak. (4) Theme pick as endowment — after level 2, offer a 3-theme choice (aurora/neon/petal) as 'your' board; this is directional evidence only, but costs near zero since 6 themes exist, and it plants the exclusive-themes Premium perk. (5) Hint demonstration before paywall — give 2-3 free hints and force-show the area-highlight hint once during level 3, so 'unlimited hints' on the paywall references a felt experience (aha-before-paywall; addresses the 55%-day-0-cancel problem by demonstrating subscription value in-session). (6) Paywall placement — after level 3's victory burst + checklist hitting ~80%, show the multi-page paywall; include a Blinkist-style transparency page ('3 days free — we'll remind you before it ends') which lifted trial starts +23%; keep loss-framing OUT of the day-0 paywall and use it later contextually ('don't lose your 5-day streak', 'your chapter progress') once real progress exists. (7) Streak — start streak=1 visibly at end of session 1 with a granted daily reward (make players 'feel rich', no discounts day 0), and ship streak-freeze/forgiveness from launch (+48% streak length). (8) Zeigarnik cliffhanger — after the paywall (dismiss or convert), auto-start level 4 and let the session end mid-chapter with the next chapter visible-but-locked on the world map; heuristic not sized, but zero-cost. (9) Pace budget — total flow (3 levels + theme pick + paywall + level 4 start) must run 9-12 minutes with zero interstitials in session 1; interstitials before the first paywall would tax both retention and trial starts.

## animation-craft

### Резюме
Research on animation/visual craft for Maze Arrows onboarding (iOS 16 floor) yields a clear split: the flashiest SwiftUI animation APIs — PhaseAnimator, KeyframeAnimator, spring presets (.smooth/.snappy/.bouncy), and the .sensoryFeedback haptics modifier — are all iOS 17+, so on an iOS 16 floor the onboarding backbone must be iOS 16-safe primitives: withAnimation + .spring(response:dampingFraction:) (iOS 13+, can replicate all three presets), matchedGeometryEffect (iOS 14+) for hero transitions, TimelineView + Canvas (iOS 15+) for performant particles (~500 particles at 60fps via resolveSymbol batching vs ~12fps with per-particle views), and UIImpactFeedbackGenerator with prepare() / CoreHaptics for haptics. Since the game already renders gameplay in SpriteKit, the tutorial itself is best done with SpriteKit-native tools: SKCropNode dimming overlay with a cutout over the one correct piece, an SKAction.sequence looping hand-ghost tap animation, and node.speed < 1.0 on the pieces layer for a slow-motion first fly-out — all iOS 16-safe and zero new dependencies. Timing guidance converges across Nielsen, Apple HIG, and Material 3: tap feedback under 100ms (haptic fired exactly on the visual event per WWDC19 audio-haptic guidance), standard transitions 250-400ms, reward/celebration moments 500-800ms (up to ~1200ms for the level-clear fanfare), and WWDC23 spring guidance of 0% bounce as default, ~15% subtle, ~30% clearly playful, >40% avoid. Reduce Motion is no longer optional polish: Apple's 2025 Accessibility Nutrition Labels let apps declare Reduced Motion support only if parallax, multi-axis/spinning motion, and auto-advancing content are disabled or replaced (dissolve/highlight-fade alternatives when motion conveys meaning), so every fly-out, shake, and particle burst needs a reduced variant from day one. On onboarding structure, the evidence favors level-design-as-teacher over a linear coach-mark tour: Monument Valley's first chapter is the canonical example (few-word prompts, each mechanic required to solve level 1), GDC "layout as teacher" guidance says to isolate one mechanic per scenario, and contextual behavior-triggered onboarding shows ~75% completion vs ~53% for linear tours with a reported +30% paid-conversion lift — which directly serves the day-0 multi-page paywall plan, since 77% of users churn on day 1 and the first 2-3 tutorial levels must demonstrate hint + Photo Maze value before the paywall. Direct competitor Amaze GO! (Oakever) uses exactly this minimal-overlay, teach-by-first-tap pattern, confirming genre convention.

### Факты
- PhaseAnimator and KeyframeAnimator are iOS 17+ only — unusable as the onboarding backbone on an iOS 16 floor; a community backport of PhaseAnimator to iOS 13+ exists (task-based state sequencing), and the standard iOS 16 fallback is chained withAnimation/DispatchQueue or Task.sleep step sequencing [Apple Developer Documentation — PhaseAnimator; Paul Bancarel (Medium), 2025 | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/phaseanimator; https://medium.com/@bancarel.paul/swiftui-phaseanimator-backported-to-ios-13-03af4b4c44e5]
- Spring presets .smooth (critically damped, no overshoot), .snappy (slight overshoot), .bouncy (visible overshoot) are iOS 17+; on iOS 16 the same feel is achieved with .spring(response:dampingFraction:blendDuration:) (iOS 13+) — approx equivalents: smooth ≈ dampingFraction 1.0, snappy ≈ ~0.85, bouncy ≈ ~0.7 at response 0.4-0.5 [GetStream swiftui-spring-animations cheat-sheet repo; Nil Coalescing animation timing, 2024 | https://github.com/GetStream/swiftui-spring-animations; https://nilcoalescing.com/blog/AnimationTimingInSwiftUI/]
- WWDC23 'Animate with springs' gives concrete bounce guidance: 0% bounce is the versatile default, ~15% reads as subtle, ~30% is where noticeable bounciness begins, above 40% risks feeling exaggerated for UI; springs also preserve gesture velocity for free and SwiftUI completion handlers fire on perceptual (not settling) duration [WWDCNotes — Animate with springs (WWDC23 session 10158), 2023 | https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc23-10158-animate-with-springs/]
- matchedGeometryEffect is iOS 14+ — fully safe on the iOS 16 floor for hero transitions (e.g., menu hero arrows morphing into the first tutorial board, or a piece flying from board into the paywall's 'unlimited hints' bullet) [The SwiftUI Lab — MatchedGeometryEffect Part 1 (Hero Animations), 2024 | https://swiftui-lab.com/matchedgeometryeffect-part1/]
- TimelineView + Canvas (both iOS 15+) is the iOS 16-safe particle engine for SwiftUI layers: pre-resolving symbols via context.resolveSymbol and batch-drawing yields ~500 particles at 60fps versus ~12fps for 500 individual views in a ForEach; .plusLighter blend mode and rendersAsynchronously add glow and off-main-thread drawing [Pavel Zak — Magical Particle Effects with SwiftUI Canvas; Dipendra Sharma — SwiftUI 240fps guide; Hacking with Swift — Special Effects, 2024 | https://nerdyak.tech/development/2024/06/27/particle-effects-with-SwiftUI-Canvas.html; https://dipendrasharma.com/articles/swiftui-240fps-performance-guide/; https://www.hackingwithswift.com/articles/246/special-effects-with-swiftui]
- .sensoryFeedback() is iOS 17+; the iOS 16 haptics fallback is UIImpactFeedbackGenerator/UINotificationFeedbackGenerator with prepare() called ahead of the event to prime the Taptic Engine and cut trigger latency — ideal for the <100ms tap-feedback budget [Use Your Loaf — SwiftUI Sensory Feedback; Hacking with Swift — UIFeedbackGenerator, 2024 | https://useyourloaf.com/blog/swiftui-sensory-feedback/; https://www.hackingwithswift.com/example-code/uikit/how-to-generate-haptic-feedback-with-uifeedbackgenerator]
- CoreHaptics (CHHapticEngine, iOS 13+) is Apple's recommended tool when you need to 'be your own haptic designer' and synchronize custom patterns with animation timelines; UIFeedbackGenerator is recommended for semantic UI events (selection/impact/notification) — use CoreHaptics for the choreographed fly-out trail and victory burst, UIFeedbackGenerator for taps [WWDC19 session 520 — Introducing Core Haptics, 2019 | https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/520/]
- Apple's audio-haptic design guidance: pair a sharp quick visual with a crisp transient high-sharpness haptic, modulate haptic intensity to match visual velocity/mass, and trigger the haptic exactly on the visual event — even small offsets feel unnatural ('harmony' principle: it should feel the way it looks) [WWDC19 session 810 — Designing Audio-Haptic Experiences; WWDC21 — Practice audio haptic design, 2021 | https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/810/; https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10278/]
- SpriteKit dimming-overlay-with-cutout is built with SKCropNode: a mask where the spotlight hole is transparent lets only the highlighted piece show at full brightness; a SwiftUI-side alternative draws full-screen rect + rounded rect with FillStyle(eoFill: true) and locates targets via anchorPreference/PreferenceKey, animating the cutout between steps with ~0.25s easeInOut [Hacking with Swift — SKCropNode tutorial; Livsy Code — Reusable Spotlight Onboarding Component in SwiftUI, 2025 | https://www.hackingwithswift.com/read/14/2/getting-up-and-running-skcropnode; https://livsycode.com/swiftui/a-reusable-spotlight-onboarding-component-in-swiftui/]
- Guided-tap choreography in SpriteKit is a plain SKAction.sequence([wait, move, scale, run{}]) loop on a hand/ghost sprite (sequence guarantees ordering; .repeatForever for the idle nudge loop); the ephread/Instructions coach-mark library pattern worth copying: forward touches inside the cutout so the player taps the real piece, not the overlay [Hacking with Swift — SKAction sequences; ephread/Instructions (GitHub), 2024 | https://www.hackingwithswift.com/read/14/4/whack-to-win-skaction-sequences; https://github.com/ephread/Instructions]
- Slow-motion first fly-out: SKNode.speed scales the execution speed of all actions on a node and its descendants (1.0 = normal, <1.0 = slow motion) — set speed ≈ 0.5 on the pieces layer only (not the whole scene) so the existing runFlyOut squash + particle trail plays at half speed while the tutorial overlay and hand cursor stay at normal speed [Apple Developer Documentation — SKNode.speed, 2024 | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/spritekit/sknode/1483036-speed]
- Nielsen response-time thresholds: <0.1s feels instantaneous (the budget for tap feedback), <1s keeps flow of thought, and direct-manipulation interactions need <200ms to preserve the feeling of continuous control; touch latencies below ~24ms are imperceptible [Nielsen Norman Group — Response Times: The 3 Important Limits, 2024 | https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/]
- Material Design 3 motion tokens (useful cross-check since Apple HIG gives no numbers): short 50-200ms for micro-interactions, medium 250-400ms for standard transitions, long 450-600ms for large/emphasized transitions; emphasized easing cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) for content entering the screen — and mobile-game-specific guidance puts reward/celebration moments at 500-800ms with spring easing, gaming transitions 400-600ms [Material Design 3 — Easing and duration tokens; Appy Pie — Mobile App Animation Guide, 2025 | https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration/tokens-specs; https://www.appypie.com/blog/mobile-app-animation-guide]
- Apple HIG Motion: prefer brief, precise animations (they feel lightweight and convey information better), make motion optional, and never use motion as the only channel for important information; when Reduce Motion is on, minimize or eliminate animation — especially spinning/scaling and depth/parallax effects that can cause nausea or dizziness [Apple Human Interface Guidelines — Motion, 2025 | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion]
- App Store Connect Accessibility Nutrition Labels (rolled out 2025) define auditable Reduced Motion criteria: to declare support, an app must disable or replace parallax/animated-blur/depth effects, multi-axis and spinning motion, and auto-advancing content when the system setting is on; if the motion conveys meaning (e.g., a piece leaving the board), Apple explicitly says do NOT remove it — replace with a dissolve, highlight fade, or color shift; must be re-verified every update [Apple — App Store Connect Help, Reduced Motion evaluation criteria, 2025 | https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria/]
- SwiftUI reads the setting via @Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion) (SpriteKit side: UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled + observe reduceMotionStatusDidChangeNotification); standard pattern is a conditional animation wrapper — e.g., .animation(reduceMotion ? nil : .spring(...), value:) or a withOptionalAnimation helper — swapping movement for opacity crossfades [Hacking with Swift — detect Reduce Motion; Create with Swift — supporting reduced motion in SwiftUI, 2024 | https://www.hackingwithswift.com/quick-start/swiftui/how-to-detect-the-reduce-motion-accessibility-setting; https://www.createwithswift.com/ensure-visual-accessibility-supporting-reduced-motion-preferences-in-swiftui/]
- Monument Valley's praised onboarding: the first level IS the tutorial — unobtrusive prompts only a few words long, every mechanic (tap path, rotate/slide environment) is required to solve level 1, and ustwo deliberately designed it so non-gamers could play without help ('Less Game, More Experience' GDC talk) [GDC Vault — Designing Monument Valley; Medium Game Design Fundamentals critical plays, 2024 | https://gdcvault.com/play/1020878/Designing-Monument-Valley-Less-Game; https://medium.com/game-design-fundamentals/critical-play-monument-valley-696efabf4e2e]
- GDC level-design guidance ('layout as teacher', Celeste GDC 2017 workshop): teach implicitly by building scenarios where the player must use exactly one core technique to advance, introducing each mechanic in isolation with distractions removed — i.e., tutorial level 1 for Maze Arrows should have only one tappable-free piece so the correct move is the only move [GDC Level Design Workshop talks; Game Developer — GDC 2017 Level Design Workshop, 2017 | https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2017-level-design-workshop; https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022449/Level-Design-Fundamentals]
- Contextual, behavior-triggered onboarding measurably beats linear tours: ~53% average completion for linear onboarding vs ~75% for contextual disclosure, with a reported +30% increase in paid conversions; NN/g likewise finds upfront tutorial tours are poorly retained vs help delivered at the moment of need [userTourKit — Progressive disclosure in onboarding; NN/g — Onboarding Tutorials vs. Contextual Help, 2026 | https://usertourkit.com/blog/progressive-disclosure-onboarding; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/]
- FTUE stakes: 77% of users churn within day 1 of install (AppsFlyer data), average D1 retention is ~24%, and effective onboarding is credited with retention lifts up to 50% — the animated first session is the highest-leverage screen real estate in the app; direct competitor Amaze GO! (Oakever, same tap-the-arrow-away genre) onboards with exactly the minimal pattern: teach 'find a line with a free path, tap to slide it off' via the first board with minimal overlays [Keewano — FTUE 5 tips (AppsFlyer stat); Segwise — mobile game retention; Apple App Store — Amaze GO!, 2025 | https://keewano.com/blog/first-time-user-experience-ftue-mobile-games/; https://segwise.ai/blog/boost-mobile-game-retention-strategies; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/amaze-go/id6758326278]

### Выводы для дизайна
Concrete blueprint for Maze Arrows onboarding: (1) Build tutorial levels 1-3 as real levels in the existing GameScene, not a separate mode — level 1 has exactly one free piece (mechanic: tap to fly), level 2 introduces a blocked piece (reuse runFieldShake/runFieldFlash, but scripted so it costs no life the first time), level 3 forces a hint use (reuse highlightArea → runHintFeedback → runGhostStep), which doubles as the premium-value demo before the day-0 paywall. (2) SpriteKit tutorial layer, all iOS 16-safe: full-board dimming via SKCropNode with an animated cutout over the correct piece; hand-ghost sprite on an SKAction.sequence([wait 0.6, moveTo, scale 0.9, scale 1.0]).repeatForever idle-nudge loop that cancels on first touch; forward touches inside the cutout to the real piece. First correct tap: set piecesLayer.speed = 0.5 so the existing runFlyOut squash + particle trail plays in half-speed slow motion, restore to 1.0 after, and pair with a prepared UIImpactFeedbackGenerator.light fired exactly on touch (<100ms budget) plus a CoreHaptics continuous fade during the trail. (3) Timing system: tap acknowledgment <100ms; cutout/step transitions 250-300ms easeInOut; fly-out at normal speed 250-400ms; level-clear celebration 800-1200ms reusing runVictoryBurst + ComboBurstView with UINotificationFeedbackGenerator.success synced to the burst convergence frame; springs via .spring(response: 0.4, dampingFraction: 0.7) for playful elements ('bouncy' equivalent) and dampingFraction 0.85-1.0 for functional moves, gated behind #available(iOS 17) to use .snappy/.bouncy where present. (4) SwiftUI shell: matchedGeometryEffect (iOS 14+) hero transition from MainMenuView's 3-arrow preview into the level-1 board, and TimelineView+Canvas with resolveSymbol batching for any celebration confetti in the SwiftUI layer (500 particles at 60fps is proven headroom). (5) Ship reduce-motion variants of every beat from day one — fade-out instead of fly-out, static highlight instead of pulse, no field shake, no slow-mo — read via @Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion) in SwiftUI and UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled in GameScene, then declare Reduced Motion support in App Store Connect's Accessibility Nutrition Label. (6) Keep prompts Monument Valley-style: 2-4 words max ('Tap the arrow'), no modal text screens, and let the board layout do the teaching — tutorial completion is the top of the day-0 paywall funnel, so every extra step must fight for its life.
